# Case Study 02 — Hundi/Hawala Network Mapping

> **Synthetic case — for portfolio demonstration only. All names and figures are fictional.**

## Case Reference
`CS-HWL-02` (synthetic)

## Typology
Informal Value Transfer System (IVTS) — **Hundi/Hawala network** used to move funds outside the formal banking channel, suspected to intersect with trade-mispricing settlement.

## Background / Trigger

A tip received through a financial intelligence referral indicated that a money-service shop operating informally, referred to here as **"Hassan Currency Exchange"** (fictional), was acting as a hawala node — collecting cash deposits domestically and arranging equivalent payouts abroad through a counterpart operator, without any funds physically crossing the border.

## Investigative Methodology

1. **Surveillance and informant-based lead development** to establish the operating pattern and identify the domestic and overseas counterpart nodes.
2. **Ledger reconstruction** — informal hawala "chits"/handwritten ledgers seized and reconciled against claimed transaction dates and amounts.
3. **Call Data Record (CDR) and financial linkage analysis** to map communications between the domestic operator and the identified overseas counterpart.
4. **Beneficiary interviews** (where legally obtainable) to corroborate that payouts abroad matched domestic deposits, confirming the settlement mechanism.
5. **Cross-check against SBP's Exchange Companies framework** to confirm the operator held no valid licence for currency/remittance business.

## Red Flags Identified

- Deposits collected in **round-figure cash amounts**, structured just under the threshold requiring formal identity verification.
- No licensing under the State Bank of Pakistan's Exchange Companies Rules for currency/remittance business.
- Handwritten ledger entries using **code names/initials** rather than full legal names of remitters and beneficiaries.
- Settlement pattern showing **no actual cross-border wire transfer** corresponding to claimed overseas payouts — consistent with net-settlement between hawala operators rather than genuine remittance.
- Multiple domestic depositors linked to a small number of overseas beneficiary names, suggesting a **structured, repeat-use channel** rather than one-off personal remittances.

## Outcome / Recommendation

- Case referred for action under the **Foreign Exchange Regulation Act** and AML Act 2010 provisions covering unlicensed remittance/hawala operations.
- Recommended coordination with FMU for **financial intelligence sharing** with the relevant overseas counterpart jurisdiction.
- Recommended the domestic operator's identified bank accounts be flagged for **enhanced monitoring**, given the absence of a formal remittance licence.
